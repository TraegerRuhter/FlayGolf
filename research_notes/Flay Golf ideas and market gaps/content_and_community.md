# Golf Content & Community Landscape for Flay Golf (2024–2026)

_Researched 2026-11-06. 16 search/fetch calls. Limits: Google autocomplete (suggestqueries.google.com) and ngf.org were blocked by the session network proxy, Reddit threads did not surface in search, and no paid SEO tool was available. Search volumes are therefore NOT verified. Facts marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from background knowledge or weak sources and were not confirmed this session._

## Who dominates golf content (2024–2026), what formats work, and what is oversaturated

### Takeaway
Golf YouTube is led by personality-driven "group of friends play a match" channels (Good Good, Bryan Bros, Bob Does Sports, Grant Horvat) and equipment reviewers (Rick Shiels). The money is serious: Good Good raised $45M in 2025. Sim-specific content is a much thinner layer of reviewers and retailer blogs (Carl's Place, Breaking Eighty, Golf Simulator Videos, David Maxfield), and most of it is tied to affiliate or retail links. Course match vlogs and big-brand club reviews are saturated. Sim accessories, sim "life" (apartments, garages, dark rooms) and honest independent tests are not.

### Inferences
- **Oversaturated:** creator match vlogs ("Can I break 80", 2v2 scrambles), driver and iron "new release" reviews (Rick Shiels, MyGolfSpy, every OEM), generic swing tips, and top-10 listicles of mats and launch monitors on affiliate sites.
- **Working:** recurring series with a cast and stakes, personality over polish, merch and products as the real business model, and community events such as fan tournaments that turn viewers into participants.
- Flay cannot compete on match vlogs. It can compete on tone (haunted and anti-country-club), on its maker identity, and on the thin sim-accessory niche, where the main publishers are also sellers.

### Gaps

## Content gaps: sim accessory reviews, launch monitor explainers, budget and apartment builds, women and beginners, dark humor, maker content, golf folklore

### Takeaway
The clearest gaps are (1) **independent, test-based reviews of small sim accessories, especially tees**, where results are dominated by affiliate listicles and retailer blogs; (2) **constrained-space builds** (low ceilings, apartments, renters), where the best material sits in old forum threads; and (3) a **dark, folklore, anti-country-club tone**. Plenty of source material exists (books like *Haunted Golf*) but I found no YouTube channel owning it. Women and beginners are a real audience: off-course golf, sims included, skews much more female, yet sim content almost entirely addresses male gear enthusiasts.

### Cited Findings
- **Tee problem is real and poorly served.** Forum users improvise fixes for tees that fly off mats: tee claws screwed into the mat with a lanyard, tethered tees that "still fly off on impact" but don't get lost, rubber tees pushed up from under the mat, drilling the mat, and even rolled black duct tape. — [GSF "tee or not to tee"](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/golf-simulator-forum/simulator-builds/298033-tee-or-not-to-tee); [GSF simulator tee UK](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/skytrak/178563-simulator-tee-uk); [Sand Trap tethered tee](https://thesandtrap.com/forums/topic/95852-tethered-golf-tee-for-the-mat); [GSF tee vs fairway](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/skytrak/145748-tee-vs-fairway-with-skytrak); [GSF duct tape](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/skytrak/38701-what-turf-do-you-use-for-skytrak/page2)
- **Tee color matters for camera launch monitors.** White tees can be read as part of the ball, so black is recommended. Mats with a separate hitting strip avoid the flying-tee issue. — [Carl's Place: Best tees for a golf simulator](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/best-tees-for-a-golf-simulator)
- **"Best tee for golf simulator" results** are affiliate listicles (ThinkIndoor runs three overlapping lists: "6 Best", "7 Best", "8 Best... 2026"; MyGarageGolf; spammy "October 2026" mat lists on unrelated domains such as foliumbiosciences.com and mayfairfarms.com) plus retailers selling their own tees (Carl's Golf Tees, Fiberbuilt). None I saw showed systematic durability or launch-monitor-misread testing. — [ThinkIndoor](https://thinkindoor.com/best-tees-for-golf-simulator); [ThinkIndoor 2](https://thinkindoor.com/best-golf-simulator-tees/); [Carl's Golf Tees](https://shop.carlofet.com/carls-golf-tees); [Folium spam example](https://foliumbiosciences.com/best-golf-simulator-mats-for-hitting/); [MyGarageGolf](https://mygaragegolf.com/?p=4263)
- **Low ceilings.** A builder with an 8'3" ceiling found irons comfortable and woods possible by choking up, but no driver. They chose a thinner mat to save height. Vendor guidance is 9 ft minimum and 10–11 ft ideal (Rapsodo: the ceiling should be 6–9 in taller than your swing height). — [GSF low ceiling brain dump](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/golf-simulator-forum/golf-simulators/369339-low-ceiling-basement-simulator-build-brain-dump-of-what-i-learned); [TruGolf](https://trugolf.com/?p=976); [Golfers Authority](https://golfersauthority.com/how-to-determine-the-ceiling-height-you-need-for-a-golf-simulator/); [Indoor Golf Outlet](https://indoorgolfoutlet.com/blogs/main/ceiling-height-for-golf-simulator-dimensions); [Rapsodo](https://rapsodo.com/blogs/golf/recommended-ceiling-height-for-golf-simulator)
- **Budget builds** run about $3k–$4.5k in forum threads. Guides cite $1,000–$3,000 DIY and suggest a net plus launch monitor first, then upgrades. Content is fragmented across forums, retailer "budget challenge" posts and a small newsletter, Sim Weekly ("3 Sim Setups, 3 Budgets"). — [GSF under $3k](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/golf-simulator-forum/simulator-builds/160143-how-to-build-a-budget-simulator-under-3k); [GSF under $4500](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/golf-simulator-forum/simulator-builds/404972-how-i-built-an-incredible-home-golf-simulator-for-under-4500-usd?p=409019); [Carl's Place $3k](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/3k-budget-how-to-make-a-low-cost-golf-simulator); [Sim Weekly](https://simweekly.beehiiv.com/p/3-sim-setups-3-budgets)
- **Market size.** NGF: 48.1M Americans played golf on or off course in 2025. Off-course participants are about 37.9M, though sources disagree. Simulator and screen-golf users grew from 3.8M (2015) to 8.1M (2024). About 51% of simulator users did not play on-course in the past 12 months. I could not open the NGF PDF (blocked), so these come via secondary sources. — [GolfGuide](https://www.golfguide.com/us-golf-participation-nears-50-million/); [Colorado AvidGolfer](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/golf-continued-its-golf-nationwide-in-2025/); [NGF white paper (link, not opened)](https://ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-NGF-White-Paper-The-Golf-Simulator-Opportunity.pdf); [Alba Play](https://albaplay.com/en/insight/why-simulator-golf-is-more-than-a-trend)
- **Women and beginners.** A secondary source summarizing NGF data says women are about 28% of on-course golfers but 35% of beginners and 43% of off-course-only participants. Verify these against NGF before publishing. Venues report women using sims as a confidence-building step before playing a course. Five Iron ran free women's sim time in June 2025. - **Folklore and haunted source material exists, mostly in print:** *Haunted Golf* (Pioppi, Gonsalves, Oosterhuis: ghost stories from New England, New Orleans and British links), *Golf's Strangest Rounds* (Andrew Ward), *Weird Facts About Golf* (Steve Drake), one "Bizarre Golf History" podcast episode, and Golf Monthly's apocryphal clubhouse tales. My one search found no YouTube channel dedicated to this. — [Simon & Schuster: Haunted Golf](https://www.simonandschuster.co.uk/books/Haunted-Golf/Anthony-Pioppi/Haunted/9781599217475); [Pavilion: Strangest Rounds](https://www.pavilionbooks.com/?p=6550); [49th Shelf: Weird Facts](https://49thshelf.com/content/view/full/1319063); [Everand podcast](https://www.everand.com/podcast/588813623/Episode-40-Bizarre-Golf-History-Who-holds-the-rightful-claim-to-America-s-first-golf-club-How-did-three-different-players-lose-in-match-play-after); [Golf Monthly](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/the-game/six-apocryphal-golfing-tales-74609)
- **Review independence is murky.** The most-cited sim reviewers also sell (Breaking Eighty's store; Carl's Place reviews its own category and sells tees). Independent Golf Reviews' Carl's Place piece showed no visible disclosure in the excerpt. — [Breaking Eighty](https://breakingeighty.com/collections/home); [Independent Golf Reviews](https://www.independentgolfreviews.com/?p=53113)

### Inferences
- "Honest independent sim-accessory reviews" is a real gap, but Flay sells a tee, so it cannot be neutral about tees. The credible version is **transparent testing with published method and data, including competitor tees, and Flay's own losses**. Being open about the methodology substitutes for independence.
- Constrained-space content (apartment, rental, 8 ft ceiling, shared wall, noise) fits "sim-golf first, not sim-only" and DIY well, and is underserved outside forums.
- The haunted, folklore niche is wide open on video and fits the Bosch / 1920s aesthetic. It is also safe content: it says nothing about how the tee works.
- Launch monitor explainers (spin, smash, AoA, why mat numbers lie) exist from OEMs (Rapsodo, Garmin, Foresight blogs). **[UNVERIFIED]** Neutral, plain-English, funny explainers are rarer. This was not deeply searched.
- Women-focused sim content is a gap on the content side, not the venue side. Venues are courting women; I found no home-sim content aimed at them.

### Gaps
- Reddit r/Golfsimulator member count, FAQ and top-question frequency were not retrieved (Reddit did not surface). **[UNVERIFIED]** The sub is commonly reported at roughly 100k+ members.
- I found no evidence on dark or humor golf channels beyond TikTok comedy accounts. Whether a "haunted golf" YouTube channel exists is unconfirmed (only one search was run).
- I found no specific examples of golf build-in-public makers. See the build-in-public section.

## SEO opportunities in sim golf

### Takeaway
Long-tail sim queries ("best tee for golf simulator mat", "golf mat tee keeps flying", "low ceiling golf simulator", "golf simulator tee color") are answered by affiliate listicles, retailer blogs and spam domains, while forum threads hold the real answers. This is the classic opening for a small brand's genuinely useful, test-based pages. Search volumes could not be measured.

### Cited Findings
- SERP for "best tee for golf simulator": Carl's Place blog, a GSF thread, ThinkIndoor (three near-duplicate lists), MyGarageGolf, retailers, and spam "October 2026" lists on unrelated domains (foliumbiosciences.com, mayfairfarms.com, pwice.com). — [search evidence: ThinkIndoor](https://thinkindoor.com/?p=26381); [pwice spam](https://www.pwice.com/?p=89565); [Mayfair spam](https://www.mayfairfarms.com/?p=89445)

- For ceiling and room-size queries, vendor blogs dominate (TruGolf, Rapsodo, GolfJoy, Golfers Authority, BenQ, Austad's, Indoor Golf Outlet) alongside many thin calculator pages. — [GolfJoy](https://golfjoy.com/blogs/page/what-room-size-and-ceiling-height-do-you-need-for-an-indoor-golf-simulator); [BenQ](https://www.benq.com/en-au/knowledge-center/knowledge/measure-the-space.html); [Austad's](https://austads.com/blogs/blog/how-much-space-do-you-really-need-for-a-golf-simulator)

## Interactive tools and calculators

### Takeaway
Many small calculators exist (room size, ceiling, enclosure, cost planner), but they are thin, vendor-run or hobby projects with no authority. No tool came up for tee height, mat-and-tee compatibility, or "what can I hit with this ceiling".

### Cited Findings
- Existing tools: a Python/Node package, golf-sim-room-calc (157 downloads; formula of golfer height plus 2.5 ft); a GitHub Pages room calculator (room, ceiling and player height inputs); an AgentCalc "build cost and swing space planner"; golfcalculator.org simulator size calculator; Fairway-Fit "Golf Room Builder"; and a Paperform enclosure-size template. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/golf-sim-room-calc/); [GitHub Pages calc](https://mikenvogt-dev.github.io/golf-sim-calculator/golf-sim-calculator.html); [AgentCalc](https://agentcalc-cloudflare-public.pages.dev/indoor-golf-simulator-build-cost-and-swing-space-planner); [golfcalculator](https://golfcalculator-org.pages.dev/golf-simulator-size-calculator); [Fairway-Fit](https://fairway-fit.pages.dev/about); [Paperform](https://paperform.co/templates/golf-simulator-enclosure-size-calculator/)

### Inferences
- A **ceiling clearance plus "what clubs can I swing" tool**, seeded with real data (height, club length, swing plane), fills a gap and earns links from Reddit and forums.
- A **tee height guide** (club to tee height to mat thickness, in printable form) fits Flay's product and needs no disclosure of the tee's mechanism.
- A **budget build planner** with a shareable parts list is useful, but crowded with vendor tools. Differentiate with "used and DIY" options.

### Gaps
- I did not test calculator accuracy or traffic.

## Community models: Discord, sim leagues, newsletters, Reddit, clubs without courses, indoor meetups

### Takeaway
The proven lightweight model is **Random Golf Club**: volunteer-run local chapters, a strong identity, and events, which grew into a media brand with sponsors (Srixon, 2026). For sims, **SGT/GSPro Discord** shows that online leagues and scrambles are the native community format. Flay can borrow both: an online "haunted" league on existing platforms, plus occasional indoor-venue meetups, without building infrastructure.

### Cited Findings
- Random Golf Club began as a meetup at Royal Park Golf Course, Melbourne, in 2018. Volunteers hosted events in their own cities, growing to 35 active chapters globally with local social accounts. It moved coordination from Discord to an app. — [Aussie Golfer](https://aussiegolfer.com.au/meeting-the-many-outweighs-golf-with-a-few/); [Tribeza](https://tribeza.com/culture/people-of-austin/erik-anders-lang-random-golf-club-austin/)
- RGC worked with courses (NGCOA, 2024) and signed Srixon as a partner in 2026. It is described as "a global community and media platform". — [NGCOA](https://www.ngcoa.org/golf-business-magazine/2024/july-august/random-golf-club); [AccessNewswire](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/sports-leisure-and-entertainment/srixon-joins-random-golf-club-to-bring-premium-performance-to-a-ne-1154814); [Mighty Networks community](https://random-golf-club.mn.co/)
- The Simulator Golf Tour (SGT) Discord has league sections, a find-a-league channel, scramble looking-for-game channels and GSPro support. GSPro came from the SGT community (beta Aug 2020), runs on Windows only, and supports stroke, scramble, stableford, match play and other formats for up to 8 players online. — [SGT GitBook](https://simulator-golf-tour.gitbook.io/quick-start/discord); [EverybodyWiki GSPro](https://en.everybodywiki.com/GSPro_Golf)
- Group Chat Golf grew partly through fan tournaments, so turning followers into players works. — [CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/groupchatgolf/)
- Women-centered clubs are forming at bar and sim venues (e.g. THR3 JACK Minneapolis) and venues run women's nights. — [TCB Mag](https://tcbmag.com/north-loop-sports-bar-thr3-jack-hosts-women-centered-golf-club-in-minneaplis/); [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/indoor-golf-simulator-women-five-iron-womens-golf-day)
- Newsletters: The Fried Egg on Substack, subscriber count not public. Sim Weekly (beehiiv) shows a small sim-specific newsletter niche exists. — [Fried Egg](https://thefriedegg.substack.com/about); [Sim Weekly](https://simweekly.beehiiv.com/p/3-sim-setups-3-budgets)

### Inferences
- Don't build a Discord first. With 5–10 hours a week, an empty server hurts more than it helps. Start with an **email list (the waitlist) plus a monthly "séance" scramble** run on an existing league or Discord, then add a Discord channel once there are about 50+ active people.
- A "club without a course" for sim golfers ("haunted chapter" of renters, garages and basements) is RGC's model applied to sims. I found no direct equivalent.

### Gaps
- No size data was found for TGC/GSPro league Discords or r/Golfsimulator. No NLU membership data was found.

## Building in public: examples and what made them work

### Takeaway
Evidence for golf-specific build-in-public brands is thin. The documented small-brand TikTok successes (Golf Daddy, ASSL, Golf4Home) grew through personality and humor plus product, not formal build logs. That leaves room for Flay, but the build log has to be entertaining, not just a progress diary.

### Cited Findings
- Golf Daddy (training aid) credits TikTok with building a "million dollar" brand within about 18 months. This is self-reported in a promotional piece. — [Sportsdigest](https://sportsdigest.in/?p=76785)
- Golf4Home (turf) grew with TikTok by mixing sales posts with entertaining content. — [PGA.info](https://www.pga.info/news/hebdon-expands-his-artificial-turf-business-with-the-help-of-tiktok)
- ASSL Golf built an audience on trick shots and humor rather than instruction. — [Audible podcast listing](https://www.audible.co.jp/pd/B0FMR22HVG)

### Inferences
- What tends to work, based on the cases above plus general maker-community knowledge **[UNVERIFIED as golf-specific]**: show failures (broken prototypes), keep a recurring numbered format ("Build Log #12"), invite the audience to decide things (colors, names), and keep the sell light.
- Patent constraint: build-log content should show *process* (printing, failure piles, test rigs, durability counters) and *outcomes* (hit counts, ball data) while hiding *mechanism*: cropped or blurred geometry, cutaway shots avoided. "Prototype #7 survived 412 drives" reveals nothing.

### Gaps
- I found no verified golf maker who grew mainly through build-in-public. Non-golf examples (3D printing YouTubers, indie hardware on Kickstarter) were not researched in this session.

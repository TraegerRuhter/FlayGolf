# Business Models, Sales Channels and Launch Playbooks for Flay Golf

Scope: low-budget models, channels and launch playbooks for a solo 3D-printed (TPU) golf accessory and dark-aesthetic merch brand. It is US-focused and uses 2024–2026 numbers where found. Research ran on 2026-11-06. Most fee figures come from third-party blogs because official pages were not fetched, and any figure not checked against a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED. The tee mechanism was not researched, as instructed. Note: www.uspto.gov is blocked in this session's network settings, so the official USPTO fee PDF could not be fetched directly.

## Channels: marketplaces, maker platforms, crowdfunding, social commerce, B2B and affiliate

### Takeaway
At about 10% all-in, Etsy is the cheapest test channel with built-in search demand, but Offsite Ads can take 12–15% more on attributed sales and become mandatory above $10k a year. TikTok Shop (about 6%, possibly 8% from Aug 2026) has the lowest marketplace fee. Amazon Handmade (15%) costs more but waives the $39.99/month plan. The biggest under-used B2B opening is the fast-growing indoor sim venue market: about 1,500+ US simulator businesses, roughly triple 2022. Golf-tee crowdfunding campaigns in the results raised only a few thousand dollars, so Kickstarter works as a pre-order and validation tool, not a windfall.

### Cited Findings
**Etsy**
- Etsy fees: $0.20 listing fee, a 6.5% transaction fee charged on item price plus shipping, and US payment processing of 3% + $0.25 — [Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained-2025/); [eDesk](https://www.edesk.com/blog/etsy-seller-fees)
- Offsite Ads: 15% fee for smaller shops and 12% for larger shops on sales from an ad click within 30 days. Shops under $10,000 in trailing 12-month sales can opt out; shops above $10,000 cannot — [Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-fees-explained-2025/); [Bootstrapping Ecommerce](https://bootstrappingecommerce.com/how-much-commission-does-etsy-take/)
- All-in cost estimate of about 9.75–10.5% per sale without Offsite Ads, and about 22–25% on ad-attributed sales (third-party estimates) — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/fees/etsy-fees-calculator-etsy-2025-guide-for-sellers-what-i-learned-after-running-3-etsy-shops); [eDesk](https://www.edesk.com/blog/etsy-seller-fees)
- Golf-themed 3D-print sellers exist on Etsy. One Patreon creator (Best3DPrints) says they launched an Etsy shop selling physical prints of their mostly golf-themed designs. No sales count was given — [Patreon: Best3DPrints](https://www.patreon.com/cw/Best3DPrints)
- A sponsored family business running 4 Bambu P1S printers had "nearly 500 sales" on Etsy — [All3DP](https://all3dp.com/4/this-11-year-old-used-her-savings-for-a-bambu-lab-printer-now-its-a-viral-family-business/)

**Amazon Handmade**

Products need the Handmade classification, and Amazon lists no other listing or processing fees — [Amazon Handmade (official)](https://sell.amazon.com/programs/handmade); [Craftybase](https://craftybase.com/blog/2019/06/20/how-to-apply-for-amazon-handmade)

**TikTok Shop**

A refund admin fee of 20% of the referral fee, capped at $5 per SKU, applies from May 15, 2025. Affiliate creator commissions are charged on top — [Printful](https://www.printful.com/blog/tiktok-shop-fees); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/calculate-profit-tiktok-shop/); [AdvertisePurple](https://www.advertisepurple.com/?p=11539)
- One 2026 source reports that TikTok raised the standard US referral fee from 6% to 8% for most non-food categories on Aug 4, 2026 (UNVERIFIED, single source) — [FastMoss](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/tiktok-shop-seller-costs-in-the-us-2026-fees-creator-commissions-fulfillment-returns-real-profit/)

### Inferences
- At this stage Etsy is a search-demand test, not a home base. Opting out of Offsite Ads while under $10k a year keeps fees near 10%. The own site plus Stripe Payment Links keeps margin and the email list.
- TikTok Shop's low fee and creator affiliate system suits a visual, "it stands on a mat" product demo better than any other channel. Short-form video is the cheapest way to show it.
- Selling STLs of the core tee would hand the design to competitors. A better digital play is non-core files (accessories, holders, display pieces) or a commercial-license tier, which keeps physical tees as the moat.
- Golf-tee crowdfunding caps out around $3k–9k in the examples found. Kickstarter is worth running only as a pre-order vehicle with a pre-built email list (Kit waitlist), not for discovery.
- Sim venues are a concentrated, fast-growing B2B audience (1,500+ US locations) with a real need, since mats are not designed for real tees. A custom-branded tee is an obvious wholesale and promo item. No source confirmed how much venues spend on consumables.
- Sim retailer affiliate programs (5–10% on $10k+ sims) can pay more per conversion than selling tees, if Flay Golf creates sim-setup content.

### Gaps

## Revenue models: direct, consumables, subscriptions, digital vs physical, licensing, B2B, drops and pre-orders

### Takeaway
The golf accessory brands that grew from small starts (e.g., Pins & Aces) mixed online DTC (about 80% of sales) with wholesale to big-box golf retail later on. For a TPU product, replaceable tops (consumables) and custom-branded B2B tees for venues and events offer the best margin per hour. Limited "drops" and pre-orders fit a dark-aesthetic merch brand and a capacity-limited single printer.

### Cited Findings
- Pins & Aces (founded 2017–2018 by Nicklaus Mertz and Jonathan Major, with Alex Bard) started from a perceived gap in fun headcovers. It runs about 80% of sales online through DTC and later added wholesale with Golf Galaxy and PGA Tour Superstore — [Colorado AvidGolfer](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/aced-it-pins-and-aces/); [Digital Journal](https://www.digitaljournal.com/sports/how-pinsaces-golf-co-brings-fun-and-ingenuity-to-the-golf-course/article); [Pins & Aces About](https://pinsandaces.com/pages/about-us)
- Pins & Aces markers are cast steel with oven-cured enamel, and the range expanded from headcovers into apparel, towels, markers and novelty items. Ball markers work as a low-cost entry item that sits alongside the main products — [Colorado AvidGolfer](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/aced-it-pins-and-aces/)
- Podcast case studies of small golf accessory founders: Boring Ball Markers (The Hole Story) and Behind the Golf Brand with Pins & Aces and OnPoint Golf — [The Hole Story](https://theholestorypodcast.buzzsprout.com/2163311/episodes/17165131-from-boring-to-brilliant-the-story-behind-boring-ball-markers); [Behind the Golf Brand #143](https://behindthebrand.buzzsprout.com/1071406/episodes/16990327-143-pins-aces-nicklaus-mertz-liam-johnson); [Behind the Golf Brand #99 OnPoint](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1071406/episodes/12898281-99-onpoint-golf-briston-peterson-founder)
- Patreon STL creators use a "merchant license" tier that lets subscribers sell prints, which is a recurring licensing model — [Patreon: Best3DPrints / Modelu3D](https://www.modelu3d.co.uk/pages/patreon)
- One 3D-printed decor seller found that 20% of products produced 80% of revenue, running the shop at 10–15 hours a week — [3DPrintedDecor](https://www.3dprinteddecor.com/make-money-selling-3d-printed-decor-case-study-2026/)

### Inferences
- Consumables: tee tops wear out and get lost, so a "refill pack" (e.g., 5 or 10 tops) builds repeat purchase at near-zero marginal cost. It also suits Stripe Payment Links and later Shopify subscriptions.
- B2B custom-branded tees in venue colors or with logos for sim venues and corporate leagues or outings move larger orders per sale. This is the likeliest path to full-time income from a single SKU.
- The 80/20 decor finding suggests launching a few variants, measuring, then cutting SKUs ruthlessly.
- Avoid selling the core tee STL. A paid "license to print for your venue" for B2B accounts is a possible later model (speculative).

### Gaps
- No published conversion or retention numbers were found for consumable refill subscriptions in golf accessories.
- Revenue figures for Pins & Aces and Boring Ball Markers were not found.

## 3D-print production economics: TPU cost, farm scaling, injection molding, outsourcing, QC

### Takeaway
TPU costs about $23–34/kg in the US, so material per small tee costs cents. Labor and print time are the real limits. Bambu A1-class farms scale cheaply (CruiseCup reached about 160 A1s and $808k revenue). Injection molding pays off only at thousands to tens of thousands of parts, and TPU-type flexible molding needs a fixed design first.

### Cited Findings
- Bambu Lab TPU 95A HF: about $33.86/kg (3DJake, excluding VAT). Overture TPU 95A: $28.99/kg, with price history between $23.19 and $28.99 — [3DJake](https://3djake.com/bambu-lab/tpu-95a-hf-black); [reach.dog / Overture](https://m.reach.dog/shop/overture-3d/products/overture-tpu)
- CruiseCup grew from one basement printer to about 160 printers (mostly Bambu A1) and $808,000 revenue, run full-time by the founder plus one employee (secondhand, citing Business Insider) — [Makers101](https://makers101.com/cruisecup-3d-print-farm/)
- Cautionary math: a 6-hour print sold for €12 with €4 filament nets about €1.30 an hour after Etsy fees — [ADP Industries blog](https://adp-industries.pages.dev/blog/make-money-3d-printing-2026/) (low-quality SEO source)
- One US home-based maker reported 30–40% profit margins and $3,855 over four months — [Supercar Blondie](https://supercarblondie.com/3d-printing-business-from-home-surprising-reality)
- Injection molding break-even (Prusa): a €6,000 mold costs €0.18 per part against €0.25–0.32 printed, a break-even of about 80,000 parts. The full calculation should include downtime, machines, labor and power — [Prusa Pro](https://pro.prusa3d.com/?p=16235)
- Formlabs: a $3,600 tool gives a break-even of 13,050 parts, and over 40,000 with bulk resin (vendor analysis) — [Formlabs](https://formlabs.com/blog/race-to-1000-parts-3d-printing-injection-molding/)
- Xometry puts break-even at 250–2,000 parts depending on geometry. Rule-of-thumb sources say 500–2,000 units — [Xometry](https://www.xometry.com/resources/3d-printing/3d-printing-vs-injection-molding-breakeven/); [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/low-volume-injection-molding-vs-3d-printing-quantity-break-point)

### Inferences
- Unit cost is driven by print time and post-processing labor, not TPU. Flay should time a full plate and compute minutes of labor per tee. That labor time is the number that matters.
- Adding a second or third budget Bambu-class printer is likely cheaper and less risky than molding until volume reaches thousands of units a month. Molding also locks the design, and TPU tooling is a separate quote.
- QC: TPU stringing and dimensional fit between the two pieces would be the main defect risks. Batch fit-testing (1 in N) and a simple go/no-go gauge are typical practice (general practice, not sourced).

### Gaps
- Craftcloud and JLC3DP TPU quote prices were not researched (no budget). Get a live quote for the actual part.
- No quotes were found for TPU or TPE injection mold costs for a small two-piece part.
- Bambu farm write-ups (Bambu Farm Manager, power cost per printer-hour) were not fetched.

## Legal and ops basics for a US side business (not legal advice)

### Takeaway
Cheap must-dos: a $350-per-class trademark (base application, from Jan 2025) for "Flay Golf", a micro-entity provisional patent (about $65, UNVERIFIED final figure) to start a 12-month clock if the mechanism is novel, and $279–810/yr product liability insurance once selling to venues. LLC timing and sales tax nexus were not researched in depth.

### Cited Findings
- USPTO trademark: a single "base application" replaced TEAS Plus and Standard from Jan 18, 2025, at $350 per class, with surcharges for incomplete applications or free-form goods/services IDs — [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/us-patent-and-trademark-office-sets-2025-fee-schedule); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trademark/1549674/us-patent-and-trademark-office-sets-2025-fee-schedule)
- USPTO patent fees effective Jan 19, 2025: the basic utility filing fee is $350 undiscounted, $140 small entity, $70 micro entity. Small entity pays 40% and micro entity 20% of undiscounted fees — [USPTO fee schedule (PDF, not fetchable here)](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/USPTO-fee-schedule_current.pdf); [Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/news-insights/articles/new-uspto-patent-fees-go-into-effect-january-19-2025/)
- The proposed-rule provisional fee was $300 rising to $315 undiscounted and $60 rising to $63 micro. The final 2025 figure was not confirmed (UNVERIFIED; likely about $325 undiscounted, $130 small, $65 micro by the 40%/20% ratios) — [Prokurio](https://www.prokurio.com/uspto-publishes-changes-to-2025-patent-fees/)
- Product liability and general liability insurance: ACT Etsy plan $24.25/month or $279/yr including product liability. The Hartford's average GL is $810/yr and BOP $1,687/yr. Insuranceopedia puts small-seller premiums at $300–1,200/yr. Zensurance starts at about $500/yr. Assureful's standard limits are $1M per occurrence and $1–2M aggregate. Etsy does not require insurance — [ACT Insurance](https://www.actinsurance.com/etsy-seller-insurance); [The Hartford](https://www.thehartford.com/small-business-insurance/etsy); [Insuranceopedia](https://www.insuranceopedia.com/?p=52094); [Zensurance](https://www.zensurance.com/e-commerce-insurance/etsy-seller); [Assureful](https://www.assureful.com/seller-insurance-requirements/etsy)

### Inferences
- File the trademark early, at $350 for class 28 (sporting goods), plus $350 for class 25 (apparel) for merch. A brand name is the cheapest lasting moat.
- If the tee design is novel, file the provisional before any public post, Kickstarter or sales, because the US one-year grace period has limits and foreign rights are lost on disclosure (general patent-law knowledge, not sourced here). A design patent may suit protecting the look of the tee.
- Sim venues and corporate buyers will often ask for a certificate of insurance. A $279–500/yr policy is the realistic trigger cost once B2B begins.

### Gaps
- Design patent fees (filing, search, exam, issue) could not be fetched because uspto.gov is blocked.
- LLC filing costs by state, sales tax marketplace-facilitator rules, and economic nexus thresholds were not researched. Marketplace-facilitator laws generally mean Etsy, Amazon and TikTok collect sales tax for the seller, UNVERIFIED. Stripe Tax and SBA guidance were not checked.
- Stripe Payment Links fee (assumed 2.9% + $0.30, UNVERIFIED) and Kit free-tier subscriber limits were not checked.

## Case studies, pricing psychology, and 25+ ranked ideas for Flay Golf

### Takeaway
The documented paths are (a) a viral niche product that scales on cheap Bambu farms (CruiseCup, $808k) and (b) a fun golf brand that went DTC first, then into wholesale (Pins & Aces). Realistic solo side-income evidence sits in the low thousands per month. The ideas below are ordered from cheapest and easiest to most costly and effortful.

### Cited Findings
- CruiseCup: about 160 printers, $808k revenue, 2 people — [Makers101](https://makers101.com/cruisecup-3d-print-farm/)
- A Made-in-USA 3D-printed aquarium habitat brand sold on Amazon FBA was listed for sale with 55% TTM revenue growth, showing that a single-niche 3D-print brand can be sold as an asset — [BizQuest](https://www.bizquest.com/business-for-sale/made-in-usa-3d-printed-aquarium-habitat-fba-brand-55-ttm-rev-growth/BW2520852/)
- UK maker: about £400 a month from Etsy personalised planters, with realistic side income put at £100–400 a month — [ADP Industries](https://adp-industries.pages.dev/blog/start-3d-printing-business-2026/) (low-quality source)
- Decor case study: 10–15 hours a week, with 80% of revenue from 20% of products — [3DPrintedDecor](https://www.3dprinteddecor.com/make-money-selling-3d-printed-decor-case-study-2026/)
- Pins & Aces: DTC first (80% online), wholesale later — [Colorado AvidGolfer](https://coloradoavidgolfer.com/aced-it-pins-and-aces/)

### Inferences — pricing psychology and bundles (general practice, not sourced in this pass)
- Use a 3-tier "good/better/best" structure (single tee, 3-pack, "Bay Pack" of tees plus spare tops) so the middle tier anchors. Bundle merch (sticker or ball marker) for free-shipping thresholds. Price the tee as a premium accessory, not a commodity tee, because the comparison set is sim-mat tees and rubber tee holders.

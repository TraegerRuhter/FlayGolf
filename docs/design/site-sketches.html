Recommendation

Build Plate I first, then borrow from the others

Plate I takes the page you already have further, and it matches the brand doc's own description of the logo: a specimen plate on a dark wall. Its four lettered callouts give the best pieces of the other concepts a place to go.

  1. A. The hide (About): the struck-through dress code and "Rules of the Club" FAQ from The Card.
  2. B. The nails (Build Log): the Field Journal, with the failed prototypes gallery from The Cabinet.
  3. C. The core (Waitlist): the signing copy and numbered tag from The Register, plus a one-line drop schedule from The Almanack.
  4. D. The margin (Tools): "What ails your bay?" from the Consulting Room.
Week 1Fix contrast and fonts on the current page.
Wk 2–3The plate with its clickable letter legend.
Wk 4–6Build log.
Wk 7–12Tee height chart, ceiling calculator, tools hub.
LaterReal numbered signups, countdown and capped Stripe link, once the patent question is settled. The Triptych becomes the page for the first dark limited drop.
CONCEPT 01

Plate I

The home page is one anatomical engraving, and its lettered legend is the site's menu.

Based on
Anatomical atlas plates: Vesalius (1543), Albinus (1747), Gray's (1858).
Signature move
Pointing at a legend letter lights up its line on the plate, and the other way round. Each letter opens a one-line truth and a link.
Why it's useful
Explains the brand without a wall of text, sends people to each section, and keeps the waitlist one tap from the top.

CONCEPT 02

The Card

A 1920s club scorecard, amended by the people who were never let in, is the site map.

Based on
1920s clubhouse scorecards, local rules and printed dress codes, redrawn in brand type.
Signature move
"Keep your card": each section you visit gets a pencil tick. Play all nine and a hidden lore page opens.
Why it's useful
The whole site at a glance, honest about what isn't built yet ("Ground under repair"). The struck-through dress code says what Flay stands for in one picture.
Effort EasySpeed cost NoneRisk Med (too close to Liquid Death)

CONCEPT 03

The Cabinet

A curiosity cabinet where each drawer is a section, and nothing sold out ever leaves the collection.

Based on
16th–17th century cabinets of curiosities and museum specimen cards.
Signature move
Drawers pull open. Sold-out and failed items stay, stamped DEACCESSIONED. Drawer VI is the "Ossuary" of broken prototypes.
Why it's useful
Browse everything from one screen. Sold-out items become part of the collection instead of a dead link, and showing failed tests builds trust.
Effort Easy–MedSpeed cost LowRisk Low–Med (empty drawers)

CONCEPT 04

The Triptych

A grey, respectable club door swings open into a full-colour Bosch-style world in three panels.

Based on
Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights: grey outer wings that open onto Eden, the garden and hell.
Signature move
"Open the doors": one click, never a scroll trigger. The panels are The Club (about), The Garden (the drop) and The Rough (log and dark drops).
Why it's useful
Mostly impact: an unforgettable first impression and a natural stage for revealing a limited drop.
Effort Med (art-heavy)Speed cost Low–MedRisk HighSave for a drop page

CONCEPT 05

Course of Flay

A nine-hole course map that drifts from parkland into Bosch country. Each hole is a section of the site.

Based on
Hand-inked course plans and yardage books from the 1920s.
Signature move
Pick a flag and a dotted ball flight draws to the green, and its hole card lights up. Tap the bog five times to find the lost ball.
Why it's useful
Golfers already know how to read a course map, and Previous/Next hole links walk them through everything.
Effort MedSpeed cost MedRisk MedUse as the 404 page

CONCEPT 06

Field Journal

The site opens on the latest dated notebook spread from the workshop, and everything else hangs off the log.

Based on
Naturalists' field notebooks and lab notebooks. It also matches the sim community's own format: the garage build log.
Signature move
Stamps thump in (HELD / FAILED / CRACKED). Anything about the tee sits behind a black redaction bar that only says "Nice try."
Why it's useful
Brings people back, earns search traffic and fills the newsletter, and it proves the DIY claim. Tracksmith runs this "story for every product" model, on the same framework Flay uses.
Effort Easy + weekly postsSpeed cost LowRisk Med (goes stale)

Try it: hover the redaction bar.

CONCEPT 07

The Register

An open club register where the only thing to do is sign it. You get back a numbered specimen tag.

Based on
Clubhouse member registers crossed with a museum accession ledger.
Signature move
Your line "inks in" and a brass tag swings over with your number. It's an honest signup number, never a fake place in line.
Why it's useful
The cleanest signup path of the ten, and a success screen people want to screenshot and share. Harry's collected almost 100k emails in a week with a similar ritual and referral rewards.
Effort Easy look · Hard real numbersSpeed cost NoneRisk Med

Try it: sign the register (nothing is sent).

CONCEPT 08

The Almanack

A monthly almanac page says what's due this month in the sim bay and when the next drop is foretold.

Based on
Farmers' and patent-medicine almanacs, and the "labours of the months" from medieval Books of Hours.
Signature move
A countdown appears only when a date is real. After 9pm or in Halloween week, the page dims to candlelight.
Why it's useful
Ties content to sim season (November to March), gives people a reason to come back monthly, and makes drops easy to follow. MSCHF and Playdate both ran on this.
Effort Easy + monthly upkeepSpeed cost LowRisk Med

CONCEPT 09

Dr. Flay's Consulting Room

The home page asks "What ails your bay?" and answers with free tools, written up as an old-fashioned consultation.

Based on
The 19th-century patent-medicine counter and a doctor's intake form.
Signature move
Three answers in, a prescription card out, with a shareable link. It never asks for your email first.
Why it's useful
The most useful of the ten. It answers the top sim-golf searches, where today's results are thin calculators and forum threads.

CONCEPT 10

Catalogue No. 1

One dense, numbered page lists everything Flay makes, tests or gives away, like an old mail-order catalogue.

Based on
Sears and Montgomery Ward mail-order catalogues: engraved cuts, item numbers, an order form. Mail order was the anti-boutique way to buy.
Signature move
A Plate/Catalogue switch flips the same items between an airy gallery and a dense print listing.
Why it's useful
The easiest to scan, and ready for a shop: each row becomes a Stripe link later, capped at the run size for numbered drops.
Effort Easy build · Med artSpeed cost Low–MedRisk LowUse once there are 5+ products

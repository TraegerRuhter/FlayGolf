# Flay Golf: plan so far

Decisions from the first planning session (September–October 2026), plus the
research behind them and the questions still open. Brand and logo details live
in [`brand/brand.md`](brand/brand.md).

## The business

- **Goal:** a side business built to grow into full-time work.
- **Going full-time:** when Flay earns about what the day job pays, or a little
  less, two months in a row. Two things to weigh when that happens: sim golf is
  likely busiest in winter, so two strong months may be seasonal, and the
  paycheck may not be the whole picture if the job also covers health insurance.
- **Time and money:** 5–10 hours a week, under $500 to start.
- **Positioning:** sim-golf first, not sim-only.

## The first product: the tee

- A two-piece tee that snaps together and stands on a simulator mat by itself,
  with no hole needed.
- It looks like a flaying board, which is where the name Flay comes from.
- One fixed height per tee, sold in a range of heights. No adjusting.
- **Status:** a printed prototype that works on one mat. Not shown publicly yet.
- **Known problem:** it sometimes comes off the mat when hit. The whole tee can
  fly, the top can pop off, or it tips; it varies. This has to be solved before
  selling, because flying off is the top complaint about mat tees.
- **Material note:** people who print sim tees report that PLA and PETG shatter.
  TPU is the plastic that holds up.
- **Patent timing:** in the US, the first public showing, sale or offer to sell
  starts a one-year clock to file. Many other countries give no grace period.
  Keep it private until the patent question is settled.

Other inventions exist but none are as far along. They become later drops.

## Research findings (September 2026)

From web search results; source pages were blocked, so treat these as leads to
verify, not confirmed facts.

### Name

- flaygolf.com was available at about $11.25 for the first year. flay.golf was
  $7.99 but renews at $55/year.
- No live "FLAY" or "FLAY GOLF" trademark turned up for clothing (class 25) or
  sporting goods (class 28).
- **Risk:** FLYY GOLF, Reg. 6003272, is a live mark for golf clothing, bags,
  headcovers and towels.
- **Risk:** a "Flay" golf brand could draw an objection over a suggested link to
  the chef Bobby Flay, a known golfer. The flaying-board origin of the name helps.
- Small FLAY clothing labels exist, including FLAY Apparel in Belfast.
- Decision: keep the name and go without a clearance search.

### Existing sim-mat tees

- Rubber tee holders that sit in a hole cut in the mat (Carl's Place, about $40
  for 3), tees that sit on top (BirTee, Daiya Tomahawk), Tee Claw, Brush-T.
- **Top complaints:** tees fly off or tip over, rubber wears out fast, fixed
  heights, mats tear around the tee hole, cheap 3D-printed tees break quickly.
- **The opening:** nothing found stands on any mat without a hole, gives
  repeatable heights, stays put, and has a cheap replaceable part that takes
  the wear.
- Dozens of free printable sim tees already exist on Printables, MakerWorld and
  Thingiverse, including two-part adjustable ones. Flay's edge has to be
  quality, fit and brand rather than the idea itself.

### Patents to have an attorney check before selling

- **US 12,097,416, "Indoor golf tee" (granted 2024):** a base with a removable
  shaft that slides to set height. The closest to Flay's tee.
- US 11,701,560 and US 12,138,514, "Height-adjustable golf tee system"
- US 2009/0325726, "Adjustable height practice golf tee"
- US 7,846,044, "Adjustable golf tee"; US 5,242,170, "Golf tee" (two-piece)

A broad patent on "a two-part sim tee" looks unlikely. Any protection would rest
on a specific new mechanism, or on a design patent for the tee's look. This is
not legal advice.

## Still open

1. What exactly makes the tee come off: which heights and clubs, and the fix.
2. Which part takes the wear, and whether spare tops sell as refills.
3. Which mat, printer and plastic the prototype uses.
4. First customer: home sim owners or indoor golf venues.
5. Testing on other mats without going public (mat samples, testers under NDA).
6. When to form an LLC, and product liability insurance.
7. How merch gets made: print-on-demand, local batches, or both.
8. Patent or speed to market.
9. Scaling production, where to sell, and pricing.

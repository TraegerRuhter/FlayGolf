# Haunt the sim bay nobody owns yet

Flay Golf's best opening is a spot no golf brand holds: the simulator, garage and apartment golfer, served by a maker brand with a fine-art macabre look. The new-wave golf brands are almost all apparel-led and aimed at the course. The dark golf goods that exist are neon skulls and Halloween novelties. Sim golf itself has hardware and software but almost no accessory or lifestyle culture of its own. The research behind this report points to a clear order of work. First, protect the tee and fix the fly-off problem in private. Then build an audience with content that never shows the tee: test-based tee and mat explainers that win weak search results, haunted golf folklore shorts for reach, and an email list. After the patent question is settled, launch the tee as a numbered, limited, collectible run in the disc-golf and keyboard mold, and add cheap printed markers, chips and sim-bay organizers around it. Below are 130 deduplicated ideas, triaged against a $500 budget and 5–10 hours a week. About a third of them cost nothing and can start this month. Many market figures come from search-result summaries, because NGF, USPTO, Reddit and several forums were blocked during research. Those figures are marked, and they must be checked before any of them goes public.

## Off-course golf outgrew the clubhouse, and nobody branded it

US golf hit a record **48.1M participants in 2025**. Off-course participants (**37.9M**) outnumbered on-course participants (**29.1M**) for the fourth year in a row. About **two-thirds of new green-grass golfers now start off-course**. All of these numbers are search summaries of NGF releases, because ngf.org was blocked during research ([SGB Online](https://sgbonline.com/exec-golf-participation-achieves-another-record-high-in-2025/); [Golf Business News](https://golfbusinessnews.com/news/management-topics/national-golf-foundation-reports-continued-participation-growth-in-the-us)). The simulator slice is growing fastest. Simulator and screen-golf users rose from **3.8M in 2015 to 8.1M in 2024** ([NGF white paper PDF](https://ngf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-NGF-White-Paper-The-Golf-Simulator-Opportunity.pdf), search summary). A separate NGF-attributed figure of **6.2M in 2023, up 73% on 2019**, conflicts with that and needs reconciling ([IBJ](https://www.ibj.com/articles/indoor-golf-venues-take-off-with-serious-casual-players)). One secondary source says about **51% of sim users haven't played a course in the past year** ([Alba Play](https://albaplay.com/en/insight/why-simulator-golf-is-more-than-a-trend), unverified). Only about **25% of new or returning golfers become regular spenders** ([pulse.bot GBR summary](https://www.pulse.bot/sports-business/blogs/gbr-wednesday-golfs-growth-story-deepens-ngf-sees-21-million-ready-to-play-as-the-ra-targets-22-mill-0187bfeb-9c02-4f97-85d2-762addbffed4/)). "Sim-golf first, not sim-only" fits this exactly. Newcomers enter through screens, and most of them are lost before they settle into the game.

The physical market has moved toward small rooms. Topgolf's same-venue sales fell **9% in 2024**, and Callaway sold 60% of it at a **$1.1B valuation, about half the 2020 price** ([MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/topgolf-sold-to-private-equity-for-1-1-billion/)). Meanwhile Five Iron launched a 3–5-room, 24/7 "Studio" format in September 2026 ([First Call Golf](https://www.firstcallgolf.com/industry-news/release/2026-09-30/five-iron-golf-launches-five-iron-studio-a-new-smaller-footprint-franchise-model)), and NGF counts **more than 1,500 US simulator venues, nearly triple the 2022 count** ([BusinessNC](https://businessnc.com/the-most-popular-way-to-play-golf-is-no-longer-strolling-traditional-fairways-and-greens/)). At home, the Garmin R10 (**$499.99**) and Square Golf (**$699.99**) created a sub-$1,000 launch-monitor tier ([Stymie](https://stymie.golf/launch-monitors/vs/square-golf/launch-monitor/garmin/approach-r10)). Garages, basements and apartments are now golf rooms, and they all share the same problems regardless of which launch monitor they use: tees on mats with no hole, worn hitting strips, alignment, stray-shot damage, noise and clutter ([GSF wishlist thread](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/play-golf/equipment/340072-what-equipment-do-you-whish-existed-or-was-better?p=366618)). Global simulator market estimates cluster around **$2B for 2025 at 7–9% CAGR**. Those come from paid market-research press releases with conflicting definitions, so treat them as unverified ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/golf-simulator-market-report)).

### Three under-owned lanes overlap

The white space sits where three unclaimed things meet. **First, sim golfers have no lifestyle brand.** The well-funded new-wave brands each own a tribe: Malbon has streetwear, with about **$28M raised in 2025** ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/flashy-golf-apparel-brand-malbon-notches-28-million-raise/)); Eastside has Black culture; Good Good has YouTube, with a **$45M raise** ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/20/good-good-golf-investment-peyton-manning/)); Bad Birdie has loud prints; Metalwood has 90s nostalgia. All of them are apparel-led and aimed at the course. Sim vendors such as Carl's Place and Rain or Shine are retailers, not culture brands. **Second, fine-art macabre is empty.** Skull golf is crowded but pop and neon. Swag's skull covers run **$125** with one-per-customer limits ([Swag](https://swag.golf/products/rainbow-skull-driver-cover)), and Pins & Aces sugar-skull covers sit near **$60** ([PGA Tour Superstore](https://pgatoursuperstore.com/mustache-sugar-skull-green-camo-driver-headcover/2000000032116.html)). No brand turned up doing Bosch, anatomical plates, sepia or 1920s memento mori. **Third, maker identity is unclaimed in golf.** Metalwood's love of old clubs comes closest, while the sim community's native content is the garage build log ([GSF DIY build](https://golfsimulatorforum.com/forum/golf-simulator-forum/simulator-builds/335070-my-diy-garage-build-summary)).

There are two caveats. Absence from search results doesn't prove absence from the market: a dark golf brand could exist on Instagram or Etsy that searches didn't surface. And demand for the dark look is inferred from Halloween roundups and skull-cover sales, not measured, which is why the landing-page A/B test (#6) ranks so high. There is also a useful side effect. Women report wanting **muted colors and subtle branding**, and only **30% are satisfied with apparel options** ([Golf Business News](https://golfbusinessnews.com/news/retail-new-products-news/women-underserved-by-uk-golf-apparel-market-says-latest-independent-research/); [MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/womens-golf-survey-results/)). Flay's bone, ash and umber palette already fits that preference, so the brand reads as more than a male goth niche.

### Pricing sets the frame: lifestyle accessory, not commodity tee

Commodity tees sell for about **$0.10–$0.33 each** ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/43110850)). Branded accessories sell at very different prices. Pins & Aces markers are **$14.95–$19.95** and its towels **$34.95** ([Pins & Aces](https://pinsandaces.com/collections/doorbusters)); the median golf Etsy shop listing is **$18.75** ([MakerWords](https://makerwords.com/shop/gamedaygolfgear)); Swag skull markers run **$66–$100** ([Swag](https://swag.golf/products/pink-skull-ball-marker)). The plan cites Carl's rubber holders at about **$40 for 3** (search summary, unverified). Flay's tee competes with sim tee holders and lifestyle markers, not with wooden tees. Production cost isn't the constraint: TPU runs **$23–$34/kg** ([3DJake](https://3djake.com/bambu-lab/tpu-95a-hf-black)). Print and finishing time per part is the number that decides whether a product pays, and one cautionary example netted about **€1.30 an hour** after Etsy fees ([ADP Industries](https://adp-industries.pages.dev/blog/make-money-3d-printing-2026/), low-quality source).

## Other sports already invented what golf lacks

Almost every comp started as a hobbyist building the thing they wanted. The mechanics that carried those brands are cheap: rotating limited graphics on a consumable, numbered and pre-funded runs, a brand that behaves like a club, and standard challenges that let strangers compare scores. Golf uses these at the premium end (Malbon x Bettinardi covers sold out with a one-per-person limit, per [Golfio](https://www.golfio.com/products/bettinardi-x-malbon-limited-edition-gangster-buckets-creme-plaid-headcover)), but barely at all on cheap consumables, and not at all for sim golf.

## Product and design concepts the owner can picture

These concepts use Flay's palette (bone, ash, ochre, rust, sepia, umber, with one teal accent) and Pirata One. Each one keeps the Bolton guardrails: no human flayed figure, no pink or red field, and teal as the only bright color. Prices are suggestions anchored to the comps above. Unit costs are the notes' estimates, not quotes.

**The tee, as an object (post-filing).** No mechanism appears in the packaging or copy. The packaging tells the story instead. The tees ship in a **Reliquary card**: a kraft or bone stock folder printed as a 1920s club-issue envelope, with a die-cut window showing the tees laid like relics, a "Specimen No." stamp, and the Pathology Report certificate on Run #000. Colorways are named like pigments: **Bone**, **Ash**, **Peat** (umber), **Ochre Rot**, **Rust** and **Reliquary Teal**. Each is checked against a camera launch monitor, and the back of the card says plainly why Flay avoids white ([Carl's](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/best-tees-for-a-golf-simulator)). The material is TPU, because forum makers report that PLA and PETG shatter. A starting price structure: single tee **$9–12**, 3-pack **$22–26**, Bay Pack **$39** with a sticker or marker. The comparison is Carl's holders at about $40 for 3 (unverified) and $15–20 lifestyle accessories. Confirm with the labor costing (#4) before publishing any price.

**Specimen markers and the pinned card.** A 25 mm ball marker printed in PETG or resin, carrying a raised relief of a numbered specimen seal, a bestiary creature, or later the flayed ball. It gets a bone or sepia wash with a teal fleck in the recess and a magnet back. Each is mounted on an entomology-style card with a mock Latin name ("*Pila flagellata*, var. teal") and a run number. Cost is about **$1.50–$3** plus about $0.50 for the card; price **$18–25** loose or **$28** carded. That sits between $9 generic markers and $66+ Swag ([Sostter](https://sostter.com/products/sugar-skull-golf-ball-marker); [Swag](https://swag.golf/products/8-bit-skull-marker)).

**"Indulgence" chips and the Seven Deadly Sins set.** Composite 39–40 mm poker-chip markers printed full color from ChipLab or Canterberry at a **25-chip minimum** ([Canterberry](https://www.canterberrygifts.com/poker-chip-dxfp/)). ## Content that earns trust while selling a tee

Flay's content plan has to work around an obvious conflict: the most valuable gap is honest testing of sim tees, and Flay sells a sim tee. The research is clear that this gap is real. Results for "best tee for golf simulator" are three near-duplicate ThinkIndoor lists, retailer blogs, and spam "October 2026" pages on unrelated domains ([ThinkIndoor](https://thinkindoor.com/?p=26381); [pwice spam](https://www.pwice.com/?p=89565)). The best-known sim reviewers also sell products ([Breaking Eighty](https://breakingeighty.com/collections/home)). Flay can't claim independence. It can claim **openness about method**, which this market lacks.

### Five rules keep Flay's tee tests credible

Publish the test protocol (rig, club, swing robot or human, counts, mat, launch monitor) before the results, and never change it after seeing them. Include competitors Flay admires and report where they win; the Tee Graveyard (#36) should show at least one category Flay loses. Publish raw hit logs as a downloadable sheet so anyone can re-check the numbers. Disclose on every page that Flay makes a tee, and put affiliate links behind the same disclosure (#22, #35). Invite outside replication by sending the test kit to a forum regular or a sim YouTuber to rerun (#40). Until the patent is filed, publish competitor-only tests and explainers, and hold Flay's own entry back. That is a credibility asset: the first round of data arrives before Flay has anything to sell.

### Reviews, explainers and tools, in order of payoff

The early engine is three pieces that never show the mechanism: the tee-flying pillar (#8), the tee color explainer (#9) and the height chart (#7). Each targets a query where the current answers are forum threads. Next come the data pieces that build authority: the Tee Graveyard, the misread test (#37) and the sim accessory autopsy (#126). Plain-English explainers with a ghostly illustrated voice (#49, #50) cover launch-monitor numbers and why mat readings flatter fat shots. For reach, the haunted folklore shorts (#11) and the 1920s-golfer skits (#56) follow what works for small golf TikToks, where humor and personality beat instruction ([Sportsdigest](https://sportsdigest.in/?p=76785)). The interactive tools fill a real hole. Existing room calculators are thin hobby or vendor projects ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/golf-sim-room-calc/); [golfcalculator](https://golfcalculator-org.pages.dev/golf-simulator-size-calculator)), and none of them answers "what can I swing under this ceiling" or "which tee height on my mat". The ceiling tool (#47) and the height chart (#7) are the two to build. The budget planner and launch-monitor chooser (#127) are crowded and come later.

## Risks to manage before anything goes public

**Patent timing is the gating risk.** In the US, any public showing, sale or offer to sell starts a one-year filing clock, and foreign rights are typically lost on disclosure. This report's sequencing treats every tee demo, pre-order, refill pack and mix-and-match part as "post-filing", because refills and mixable parts reveal the tee's structure even when the mechanism isn't described. The notes found US 12,097,416 ("Indoor golf tee", a base with a removable height-setting shaft) to be the closest prior patent, and K2 Golf's design patent has already forced at least one MakerWorld takedown ([MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/models/957732)). Build-log content can show process and outcomes ("Prototype #7 survived 412 drives") but never geometry or cutaways. Provisional fee figures are unconfirmed because uspto.gov was blocked.

**The Bolton resemblance is a design-time risk, not a launch-day one.** House Bolton's sigil is a flayed man on an X-shaped cross on pink and red. Flay's logo already avoids the human figure and the red field. Three ideas from the notes walk back toward the sigil: the flayed-golfer figurine, the "Golfer, Flayed" anatomical shirt, and X-shaped hero objects. They're parked (#146–#148) for that reason. Also avoid GoT language, such as a "Winter is coming" cold-season campaign. Trademark adds two more risks: the live FLYY GOLF registration and a possible objection over Bobby Flay. The owner chose to skip a full clearance search; a free DIY knockout (#27) and the $350 filing (#29) are the cheap hedges. Bosch can be redrawn freely, but some museum reproductions carry restrictions ([Bridgeman](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/de/noartistknown/title/notechnique/asset/4596587)). Beksiński and the album covers are mood only. The AI reference logo can't anchor a copyright until a person redraws it.

**Several figures are soft.** NGF participation and simulator numbers (48.1M, 37.9M, 8.1M, the two-thirds on-ramp, 43% women among off-course-only players, 51% of sim users skipping courses) come from search summaries and secondary sources because ngf.org was blocked. The 6.2M (2023) and 8.1M (2024) simulator-user figures don't reconcile. r/Golfsimulator is either about 53K or 100k+ members, both unverified. Global simulator market sizes are paid press releases. Unverified business figures include Malbon's revenue, Seamus's Particl estimate, the BizQuest 66% margin claim, the Amazon tee-holder sales tracker (about 1,000 units a month), TikTok's 8% fee change, the GSPro price, the domain price, Carl's $40 for 3, CruiseCup's revenue, and every POD base price (printful.com was blocked). Blocked or unread sources also included golfsimulatorforum.com (mostly snippets), Reddit, Sim Weekly, PlayBetter, Rain or Shine, Etsy and MakerWorld listing data, and Google autocomplete. No search volume in this report was measured.

**Operational risks** round out the list. Print-hours economics can quietly pay below minimum wage. Sim demand is likely seasonal, so two strong winter months may not repeat. Blind packs need a legal check. Venue B2B will require liability insurance. Etsy's Offsite Ads become mandatory above $10k a year. Venue spending on consumables is completely unmeasured, so the B2B path rests on inference until 3–5 local lounges are tested.

## Conclusion

The research changes what Flay's first year should look like. The tee is the hero, but it can't be public yet, so the first months should go to building the conditions it will launch into: an audience earned through test-based tee content and haunted folklore, a waitlist that measures whether the dark look actually converts, and a few cheap printed objects that prove people will pay $18–25 for Flay's art before they've seen the tee. That ordering turns the patent constraint into a strength. Flay's competitor tests run before it has anything to sell, which makes its own entry more believable later.

The deeper insight from the comps is that the sim bay is a new room without a culture, the way the indoor bike trainer was before Zwift and the sim-racing rig was before its accessory makers. Disc golf and keyboards show how a cheap, swappable object becomes collectible: rotating art, numbered runs, a maker people know by name. Flay's advantage is that it can use those mechanics at one-printer scale, in a look no golf brand holds. The risk is spreading thin across 130 ideas. The discipline is to run the 27 "Do now" items, launch one numbered tee run when counsel allows, and let the 80/20 sales data decide which of the rest earn a place.
